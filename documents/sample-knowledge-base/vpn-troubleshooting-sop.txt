# Standard Operating Procedure (SOP): Enterprise GlobalProtect VPN Troubleshooting
Document ID: SOP-IT-VPN-001
Category: VPN & Remote Access
Target Audience: Employees and Tier-1 Support

## 1. Overview
This procedure outlines the diagnostic steps for employees experiencing frequent disconnects, authentication timeouts, or gateway failures when connecting to the corporate GlobalProtect VPN.

## 2. Common Symptoms & Root Causes
- Gateway Timeout (Error 504): Corporate portal is under heavy load or local ISP is throttling UDP packets on port 4501.
- Immediate Disconnection after 5 minutes: IPsec MTU packet fragmentation or stale session tokens.
- Invalid Certificate Warning: Client machine clock is desynchronized from Active Directory domain controllers.

## 3. Step-by-Step Resolution Procedures

### Step 1: Verify System Time Synchronization
1. Open Windows Settings > Time & Language > Date & Time.
2. Ensure "Set time automatically" is enabled.
3. Click "Sync now" under Additional settings. If clock drift exceeds 120 seconds, Kerberos authentication will reject the VPN session.

### Step 2: Switch VPN Tunnel Protocol from IPsec to SSL Fallback
1. Open GlobalProtect client from the system tray.
2. Click the hamburger menu (top right) > Settings > Troubleshooting.
3. Check the option: "Prefer SSL Tunnel over IPsec (TCP Port 443)".
4. Reconnect to the primary gateway: `vpn-primary.corp.intellidesk.com`.

### Step 3: Clear Stale Credentials and Cache
1. Disconnect the VPN.
2. Open PowerShell as Administrator and run:
   ipconfig /flushdns
3. In GlobalProtect, click Sign Out, then sign in with your corporate email and multi-factor authenticator token.

## 4. Escalation Policy
If all 3 steps fail, escalate the issue to the Network Operations Team (Tier-2). Request a temporary software profile reset in Palo Alto Panorama.
